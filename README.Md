"The limitations of fixed basis functions include:\n\n1. **Limited Success**: The traditional approach of using fixed basis functions, determined by domain knowledge and trial-and-error, often met with limited success. This is because the chosen basis functions may not effectively capture the underlying structure of the data.\n\n2. **Curse of Dimensionality**: In high-dimensional spaces, the number of independent coefficients can grow rapidly, making polynomial regression and other fixed basis function approaches unwieldy. This phenomenon is known as the curse of dimensionality, which affects the performance of models as the dimensionality increases.\n\n3. **Inflexibility**: Fixed basis functions do not adapt to the specific characteristics of the data. They are chosen independently of the problem being solved, which can lead to poor performance, especially in complex or high-dimensional datasets.\n\n4. **Data Sparsity**: In high-dimensional spaces, data points may be sparse, leading to empty cells in partitioned spaces. This can result in test points being unclassifiable if they fall into these empty regions.\n\n5. **Inadequate Representation**: Fixed basis functions may not adequately represent the data manifold, especially when the data is confined to a low-dimensional manifold within a high-dimensional space. This can lead to ineffective modeling of the relationships within the data.\n\nOverall, these limitations highlight the need for more sophisticated, data-driven approaches that learn basis functions from the training data rather than relying on fixed, predetermined functions."
